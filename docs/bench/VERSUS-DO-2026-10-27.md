# Convex vs bunvex on DigitalOcean: three droplets (27 Oct 2026)

The first comparison where neither server shares its box with the database or the load generator. In the
[VPS report](E2E-VPS-2026-10-05.md), k6, the fan-out generator and Postgres all ran on the server's 2 vCPUs. Here
each has a droplet of its own, in one region's private network:

| droplet | size | what runs |
|---|---|---|
| server | c-4 (4 dedicated vCPUs, 8 GB; Xeon 8168) | Convex, then bunvex, one at a time |
| db | c-4 (Xeon 8358) | Postgres 17 (Docker, `shared_buffers=2GB`, `max_connections=1000`), nothing else |
| load | c-4 (Xeon 8168) | k6 over HTTP, and the WebSocket fan-out with each product's own client |

c-4 is the largest size this account may create: DigitalOcean refuses the 8-vCPU sizes ("restricted, open a
ticket to increase your account tier").

- **Convex:** the prebuilt backend of release `precompiled-2026-10-26-04d2f3b`, the commit bunvex follows
  ([upstream.md](../parity/upstream.md)), with `--db postgres-v5`, default knobs. The app was deployed by Convex's
  CLI (npm `convex@1.48.0`).
- **bunvex:** main at `ae13e6dd`, one process (`bench/server.ts`), `PERSISTENCE=postgres`.
- **Setup shared by both:**
  - the same functions: `bench/functions.ts`, given to Convex with its imports rewritten (`bench/cloud/convex-app.ts`);
  - the same seed (100 000 items, 1 000 counters) on a fresh database;
  - the write throughput limit lifted (`MAX_BYTES_WRITTEN_PER_SECOND`).
- **Harness:** `bun bench/cloud/versus.ts all`. Raw data:
  [bench/results/cloud/2026-10-28-02-12-versus-7cd25d51](../../bench/results/cloud/2026-10-28-02-12-versus-7cd25d51/).
  The harness commit (`7cd25d51`) differs from main only in bench/.
- **HTTP:** 8, 32 and 128 VUs, 3 runs of 20 s per point. The best VU count is kept, the median of its runs.

## HTTP (req/s · p99)

| scenario | Convex | bunvex | bunvex ÷ Convex |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| query, cached | 9 005 · 32 ms | 8 457 · 32 ms | 0.9× |
| query, uncached | 591 · 161 ms | 2 061 · 11 ms | 3.5× |
| insert, durable | 576 · 334 ms | 2 655 · 82 ms | 4.6× |
| increment, 1 000 keys | 484 · 219 ms | 984 · 82 ms | 2.0× |
| increment, 1 key | 95 ⚠ · 1.1 s | 118 ⚠ · 1.1 s | 1.2× |
| action (query + mutation) | 262 · 679 ms | 2 005 · 90 ms | 7.7× |
| mix 90/10 | 756 · 430 ms | 1 936 · 478 ms | 2.6× |

⚠ = more than 1 % errors (retries spent on the hot key: Convex 7.8 %, bunvex 6.3 %).

**CPU on the server box** at those points (cores busy, of 4):

| scenario | cached | uncached | insert | incr. 1 000 | incr. 1 | action | mix |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Convex | 3.07 | 3.12 | 3.05 | 2.97 | 0.80 | 3.05 | 3.01 |
| bunvex | 0.84 | 0.91 | 0.69 | 0.79 | 0.24 | 0.73 | 0.90 |

The database droplet was never the limit: at most 1.01 cores, and below 0.35 everywhere else.

What the tables say:

- **Convex uses the 4 cores and bunvex uses one.** Convex reaches ~3 busy cores in every scenario but the hot key.
  bunvex is one process with one JS thread, and never passes 0.91 cores.
  - Per core, bunvex does 3.4× (cached) to 32× (action) the work.
  - Throughput alone undersells that gap on a 4-core box.
- **Cached reads are a tie** (0.9×). It is the one scenario where bunvex's single thread is the ceiling: Convex
  matches it with 3.6× the CPU.
- **Everything that touches storage favours bunvex** by 2× to 7.7×, with a lower p99. The exception is the mix:
  bunvex's p99 there is at 128 VUs, against 115 ms at 32 VUs for 1 902 req/s.
- **The gaps are smaller than on the 2-vCPU VPS** (uncached 4.7× → 3.5×, insert 8.1× → 4.6×, action 8.6× → 7.7×,
  mix 4.1× → 2.6×). Convex scales with the extra cores; one bunvex process does not.
  - Horizontal scaling (STUDY-184, query workers) is the work that answers this.

### Cached reads: a regression check

Convex led on cached reads here and nowhere before. So both were measured again on 28 Oct on the two boxes with
older reports, Postgres 17 on the same box:

- the same `versus-serve.ts` and `bench/load/k6.js`;
- 8, 32 and 128 VUs, 3 runs of 20 s each;
- the best VU count is kept, the median of its runs (req/s, server cores).

| box | scenario | Convex then → now | bunvex then → now |
|---|---|--:|--:|
| Hostinger VPS (2 vCPU EPYC 9354P, k6 on the box) | cached | 4 299 → 4 695 (1.18) | 5 925 → **8 402** (0.84) |
| | uncached | 282 → 476 (1.72) | 1 337 → 2 526 (0.98) |
| MacBook M1 Pro (8 cores, k6 on the Mac) | cached | 26 040 → 30 116 (4.59) | 27 007 → **27 047** (1.00) |
| | uncached | 1 762 → 1 437 (5.76) | 7 916 → 8 412 (1.07) |

"Then" is [E2E-VPS-2026-10-05](E2E-VPS-2026-10-05.md) for the VPS and [E2E-MAC-2026-09-29](E2E-MAC-2026-09-29.md)
for the Mac. The Mac had other work running and is the noisier of the two.

**There is no regression.** bunvex's cached reads are 42 % above the 5 Oct VPS figure and back at the 29 Sep Mac figure.
The [4 Oct rerun](RERUN-2026-10-04.md) had found them at 6 671 on the Mac; #407 fixed that.

**What decides cached reads is cores.**

- **bunvex** is one process with one JS thread.
  - It reaches the same ~8 000 req/s on the VPS and on c-4, at 0.84 cores whatever the VU count. On the Mac it
    sits at 1.00 core.
  - That ceiling is its own thread, not the network or k6.
- **Convex** spreads the same reads over every core it gets:
  - on the VPS, ~1.2 cores (k6 takes the rest), and bunvex leads by 1.8×;
  - on c-4, 3 cores, for a tie;
  - on the Mac, 4.6 cores, and Convex leads by 1.1×.
- **Every scenario that reaches storage** still favours bunvex, on all three boxes.

## Fan-out (WebSocket, 20 messages to N subscribers of one query)

N clients on the load box (`ConvexClient` from `convex/browser`, `BunvexClient` from `bunvex/browser`) subscribe to
one query. A mutation is posted every second, and a delivery counts when its result reaches a client
(`bench/cloud/fanout.ts`). The cells read: delivered · p99 delivery latency · peak server RSS.

**Splay off** (`SUBSCRIPTION_INVALIDATION_DELAY_MULTIPLIER=0`, the same knob on both):

| subscribers | Convex | bunvex |
|--:|--:|--:|
| 100 | 100 % · 32 ms · 2.2 GB | 100 % · 21 ms · 594 MB |
| 1 000 | 100 % · 172 ms · 2.7 GB | 100 % · 75 ms · 623 MB |
| 5 000 | 100 % · 931 ms · 5.4 GB | 100 % · 238 ms · 550 MB |
| 10 000 | **killed: out of memory** (7.6 GB anon RSS) | 100 % · 443 ms · 671 MB |

These servers had just run the seed and the HTTP suite, so their RSS starts high.

**Splay on** (shipped settings; a fresh process):

| subscribers | Convex | bunvex |
|--:|--:|--:|
| 100 | 100 % · 41 ms · 190 MB | 100 % · 36 ms · 89 MB |
| 1 000 | 53 % · 2.35 s · 679 MB | 53 % · 2.58 s · 99 MB |
| 5 000 | 28 % · 5.94 s · 3.2 GB | 21 % · 5.63 s · 140 MB |
| 10 000 | **killed: out of memory** | 11 % · 9.96 s · 172 MB |

- **Convex dies at 10 000 subscribers** on 8 GB, with the splay on and with it off. The kernel's OOM killer took
  `convex-local-backend` both times. On the 2-vCPU VPS it died there too, at 6.7 GB.
- **bunvex holds 10 000** with 100 % delivered (splay off) and **172 MB** (splay on).
- **With the splay on, both drop intermediate values alike** (53 % at 1 000). That is the splay's purpose
  (STUDY-08), not a loss.
- **Memory per subscriber** (splay on, 100 → 5 000 subscribers): Convex ~630 KB, bunvex ~11 KB.

## Harness notes

- The first run stalled twice when Convex was killed: its clients reconnected for ever.
  - `fanout.ts` now gives up after `TIMEOUT_MS`.
  - `versus.ts` reads the kernel's OOM kills on the server box, records the point as out of memory and skips the
    larger N.
  - Both stalled points were ended by hand in this run; the numbers above are unaffected.
- One run per configuration: the HTTP points have 3 repeats each, the fan-out points one.
